## Behavior and audiences

An award category describes an individual or team distinction. Each result has one individual or one final solution/discovery team recipient; multiple results in one category support multiple winners. These are awards, not judging scores, tournament placements, attendance or a participation ledger. Missing awards never imply competed-and-lost. `randomizer.hasPresented`, profile claims and historical mutable `uid`/passcode fields have no authority to confer credit.

Staff create categories, save staff-only drafts, publish explicitly, discard drafts, finalize an event's results, correct a published category, revoke an award, and reopen finalized results for deliberate backfill. Closed/past events can be backfilled. Finalization refuses unresolved drafts and blocks new categories, drafts and publication until reopened; audited category corrections and revocations remain possible. Revocation is terminal. A wrong recipient is corrected by revoking and issuing a replacement, rather than rewriting an issued roster. Categories themselves are immutable; creating a corrected category and moving a result preserves the published historical distinction until the explicit correction.

* Event owner, private `sessionAccess.adminEmails` coadmins and existing platform administrators manage relevant drafts and audits. `officialRoles` (including judge, mentor and organizer labels), team labels and actual team leadership never grant publishing power.
* Owners see their own published, safely attributed achievements, including records hidden from their profile. New records default to `visibleOnProfile:false`; each owner explicitly selects profile records. Staff cannot select somebody else's profile visibility.
* Other verified, nonanonymous signed-in members see only selected profile achievements. Independently, official published event results are available to verified signed-in community members and can name verified recipients even when a recipient hides their profile achievement. This audience distinction is explained in both interfaces.
* Signed-out, anonymous and unverified identities cannot use any award API or view profiles/results. A deleting/deleted recipient profile cannot be read as a member achievement. Safe event summaries anonymize unavailable identities immediately; source cleanup removes attribution durably during profile deletion.

The admin component is isolated in `src/pages/admin/AwardsTab.tsx`; one tab is added to AdminSession. `Achievements` appends to existing account/member pages without replacing profile editing/history. The event view lives at `/results/:sessionId`, linked from the participant header, admin tab and achievement cards. Views refresh on explicit request; profile achievements also refresh on window focus. API reads rejoin authoritative published sources rather than trusting stale copied summaries.

All new collections are server-only in Firestore rules, including staff reads/writes; API responses enforce the audience and allowlists. No modification of `profiles/{uid}`'s exact `{name,state}` contract. PR2 profile editor, private source, selected member projection, connections panel, invitations and the committed-profile signup metadata guard are preserved. Achievements do not read private professional/contact source documents. No new registration or participant row. Existing `participants/{participantId}` and `registrations/{sessionId}~{uid}` are reused. PR2 `memberDetails` and actual PR6 attendance/history remain independent and preserved.

| Path | Contract |
| --- | --- |

| `eventResults/{sessionId}` | `{revision:number,finalized:boolean,updatedAt:ISO}`. Missing means revision 0, open. Every staff mutation updates revision transactionally; caller must supply exact expectedRevision. |

Social deletion scrubs actor/subject attribution and associated reason while retaining action, revision, event/result references and timestamp. |

`shared/awards.ts` exports category, target, snapshot, recipient and safe summary types, input validators and the summary allowlist. Public summary fields are exactly `{id,sessionId,eventName,eventDate,categoryName,teamName,publishedAt,recipientNames}`. No contact, participant/member identifiers, arbitrary profile fields, actor or audit reason flows into community summaries. An unlinked/ineligible historical recipient is represented explicitly as uncredited rather than silently assigned to an account. Its unverified name is not projected. A team aggregate remains published with other members' credit intact when a recipient is deleted.

## Publication and concurrency trust boundaries

`server/awards.ts` installs after PR1's verified nonanonymous bearer middleware in `server/identity.ts`. Every mutation checks event/staff authority inside a transaction; labels are never consulted for permission. Cross-event category/result/team/participant references are rejected, including draft recipients. Draft recipient relationships are revalidated on publication.

Team membership is discovered from the existing participant aliases (`teamId`/`solutioning_team_id`, `discoveryTeamId`/`discovery_team_id`), scoped to the same event. Both aliases are queried and deduplicated; conflicting nonempty aliases reject publication. `allocationEligible` controls team personal-credit eligibility. All current recorded members are represented in the snapshot; staff-only/inactive members are explicitly ineligible. Individual recognition does not require the participant allocation role (e.g. a mentor can receive recognition). A historical final team with no remaining recorded members can receive an explicitly team-only award with no personal credit. There is a deliberate limit of 80 recorded members per team award to keep transactions bounded; no partial roster is silently credited.

Personal credit requires BOTH matching participant `memberId`/`uid`, the trusted exact event registration pointer and its matching `uid`/`sessionId`, an active profile and an enabled verified nonanonymous Auth account. Canonical name comes from the minimal profile. Old mutable uid/name/passcode alone cannot credit anything. Existing PR1/PR6 legacy participant IDs (including `~`, up to 256 characters) are accepted as document references without treating the ID as ownership proof; slashes and oversized references are rejected. No later legacy linking retroactively confers credit; staff must revoke and reissue after independent organizer linking if credit needs to change.

Membership, team metadata, source participant, registration pointer, profile, category, authority and results revision are read in the publication transaction. Concurrent source edits cause retry. The first successful publication stores the snapshot; later team rename/move/removal or profile rename cannot rewrite it. Correction reads that stored snapshot. The event-wide revision serializes staff operations and finalization; stale requests receive 409 and UI refreshes. Reads for event/member views and visibility changes are transactional with source/profile checks, preventing a mixed revoked/visible source response.

Award writes read actor and canonical linked-recipient profiles transactionally **before branching on team allocation eligibility**. A deleting/unavailable linked profile produces a deleted team recipient with both participantId and memberId null, including historical judge-only and inactive participants. Personal credit still independently requires matching UID, trusted registration pointer and verified Auth identity; mutable legacy UID/passcode alone never triggers a member link. Individual drafting/publication rejects unavailable profiles, and correction anonymizes unavailable credited recipients. Deleting staff retain their existing authority but later actions carry null social actor/reason attribution. This prevents new award/audit references from appearing after a deletion manifest was captured.

Independent composed-stack audit finding 2 identified that the original ineligible-recipient branch bypassed this lifecycle read and could add a participant ID outside an already captured deletion plan. The issuance branch is now reordered. The real regression covers both linked judge-only and inactive historical team members: capture the immutable plan, publish a draft absent from that plan before participant erasure, then complete the actual composed delete route. It asserts no participant/snapshot/subject-audit reference survives and peer credit remains. Both judge-only and inactive cases passed against the actual composed delete route in the cumulative API/rules suite. Separate audit finding 1 is addressed by inherited PR6 `cbe48355`: additive writer transforms re-read all shared targets before writes under the current attempt; `queueSharedContentCleanup` removes only current matching identity/attribution, preserving peer joins/leaves/new names/prose. Its module/helper/test files remain unchanged, and the cumulative harness retains `tests/shared-cleanup.test.ts` sequentially before award tests. The composed current-record regressions passed; independent focused review closed both findings.

| --- | --- |

No direct client writes. |

## Integrated durable deletion contract

`server/award-cleanup.ts` now runs in the actual `/delete-profile` route immediately after `beginProfileDeletion`, before any participant/pointer erasure or parent cleanup completion. `prepareAwardCleanup` persists award IDs first; `cleanupAttendance` captures its plan and completes its isolated work; `cleanupAwards` consumes the saved award plan; only then does existing connection/base cleanup run and the parent finish. It uses the shared `withProfileDeletionFence<T>(database, uid, attemptId, callback(transaction, jobSnapshot))` from `server/profile-cleanup.ts`, byte-identical to current PR6 followup `cbe48355` (the fence signature from `52eaa21` is unchanged). The helper reads BOTH current attempt and profile `state:'deleting'` inside every transaction; callback reads/mutations and lease renewal share that transaction. No award cleanup uses raw BulkWriter or a separately supplied state-only assertion.

1. `prepareAwardCleanup(database, uid, attemptId)` uses the fence for discovery AND persistence of `profileDeletionJobs/{uid}.awardPlan`. The plan contains `{resultIds,auditIds,achievementIds,participantIds,completed:false}`. Participant IDs come from the parent's durable manifest. All IDs are captured before identity discovery keys are erased. Retries return the existing saved plan, never replace its recovery IDs with rediscovery. `beginProfileDeletion` merge-preserves both award and attendance namespaces on takeover.
2. `scrubAwardRecord(database, uid, attemptId, type, recordId)` re-reads the authoritative saved plan AND current source within the fence. It rejects IDs outside that plan. It conditionally clears only current references to the deleted identity, preserving newer unrelated actor/subject data. Result recipient IDs/name become the deleted marker; individual targets are removed and unresolved individual drafts revoked. Team/event/category snapshots and all other members' credit remain. Relevant audit subject/actor attribution and associated reason are removed; unrelated/newer actor/reason survive when the current record no longer references the deleted identity. Only the deleted member's achievement projections are deleted.
3. `cleanupAwards(database, uid, attemptId)` awaits every saved per-record mutation, then uses another fenced transaction to set `awardPlan.completed:true`. All IDs remain in the job for retry until parent completion. Awards never writes profile lifecycle or deletes the job. The parent `createProfileCleanup.finish` verifies current attempt AND deleting lifecycle and rejects any present award/attendance plan with `completed !== true`, then alone marks deleted/removes the job after all modules succeed.
4. A worker paused before planning, before manifest persistence, during item scrubbing or before completion cannot create a job or delete new awards/projections after another attempt completes and the profile is recreated. All such writes encounter the same transactional attempt/lifecycle check. Award issuance/correction/audit writers also read lifecycle, so they cannot add fresh deleted-subject or deleted-actor references after manifest capture.

No Auth user, users/platform role, event owner, peer projection or aggregate shared team result is deleted. `queueConnectionCleanup` remains untouched and runs through the parent's fenced queue. Private professional source/member projection/contact projection cleanup from PR2 is retained.

The actual route regressions in `tests/awards.test.ts` passed in the cumulative real API/rules suite. They exercise two partial failures: a later award-source transaction fails after an earlier source is anonymized; and a historical publication-reference error fails parent cleanup after award scrubbing. Both retain manifest IDs for authenticated route retry. Additional cases cover peer credit/aggregate survival, unrelated newer audit preservation, incomplete-module completion refusal, actual profile recreation/new award issuance and stale old attempt planning/source/audit/projection/parent writes rejected without recreating a job or removing new data.

Both `await cleanupAttendance(...)` and `await cleanupAwards(...)` compose before identity erasure on final PR6 `cbe48355`. Both completion gates, attendance routes/history/metadata, current-record shared-content transforms and every cumulative suite are preserved. The shared helper/shared-content/attendance module files remain unchanged from that parent. Parent PR5 alias compatibility, synchronized assignment and phase restrictions remain while awards reject genuinely conflicting nonempty aliases.
